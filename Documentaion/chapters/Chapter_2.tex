\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{chap:literature_review}

The literature review provides a comprehensive survey of relevant research in wireless sensor networks for agriculture, plant disease detection using computer vision, low-power communication protocols, and edge AI deployment on resource-constrained platforms. The review is organized thematically to highlight how prior work motivates the contributions of this thesis and to identify gaps that Edge AI-enabled visual monitoring addresses.

\section{IoT \& LoRaWAN Vineyard Monitoring Systems}
\label{sec:iot_lorawan_vineyard}
The traditional vineyard monitoring systems are focused on environmental data acquisition through wireless sensor networks. Hnatiuc et al. developed an intelligent grapevine disease detection using IoT sensor network that integrates environmental sensors for monitoring temperature, humidity, and atmospheric conditions~\cite{hnatiuc2023intelligent}. This approach represents the foundational methodology where IoT sensors collect environmental data to predict plant health status. Next, Gawande et al. proposed early prediction of grape disease occurrence using a hybrid classifier model in association with IoT sensors, demonstrating the integration of machine learning with IoT for environmental monitoring~\cite{gawande2024early}. Their work emphasized that monitoring environmental parameters via wireless sensor networks enables the prediction of plant diseases~\cite{gawande2025analyzing}.

The adoption of LoRaWAN technology represents a significant advancement for large-scale vineyard monitoring due to its nature in long-range, low-power characteristics. Kirubakaran and Samreen proposed a Grape Leaf Diseases Identification System Using Convolutional Neural Networks and LoRa Technology, which is an early attempt to combine the CNN diseases detection with LoRa network communication~\cite{kirubakaran2024grape}. More recently, Hnatiuc et al. made a comprehensive study on IoT monitoring for early identification of diseases in grapevines, integrating AI with IoT sensor networks~\cite{hnatiuc2025aiot}. Their study reveled how the integration of AI with IoT sensor networks can enhance the diseases' detection in grapevines, but it was primarily focused on environmental data collection and did not explore the edge-based visual-based monitoring.

\section{Deep Learning Models for Grape Leaf Disease Detection}
\label{sec:deep_learning_grape_leaf_disease}


The usage of deep learning techniques in grape leaf disease detection brought some improvemet, evolving from the traditional machine learning approaches into sophisticated Neural Network architectures. Aher et al. provided comprehensive  review on deep learning for grape leaf disease detection\cite{aher2025deep}, by putting similar methods together and identifying key challenges in that domain. The early approaches were employed using the classical machine learning techniques, as demonstrated by Shantkumari and Uma, who used machine learning techniques for detection of grape leaf disease using SVM and IKNN \cite{shantkumari2023machine}. Javidan et al. further explored diagnosis of grape leaf diseases using automatic K-means clustering and machine learning~\cite{javidan2023diagnosis}, achieved reasonable accuracy but with extensive manual feature engineering.

The transition to CNN showed a significant change in detection precision and automation. Diana Andrushia et al. introduced image-based disease classification on grape leaves using convolutional capsule network architectures\cite{diana2023image}, achieving accuracy of 99.12\%. Nargotra and Sharma demonstrated grape leaf disease detection using deep learning with CNN-based approaches\cite{nargotra2023grape}, while Soni explored deep learning for accurate grape disease classification in ResNet50 architecture\cite{soni2024deep}. The evolution towards to the sophisticated architectures is evidence on Praveen et al.'s work on the novel classification approach for grape leaf disease detection based on different attention deep learning techniques\cite{praveen2023novel}, which applied attention mechanism    to enhance feature decriptions.

Since the transfer learning became the dominant strategy to deal with not enough training data problems. Vo, Kheo and Nhon et al. together they proposed fusing Xception and ResNet50 features for robust grape leaf disease classification\cite{vo2025fusing}, demonstrating that combining several pre-trained architectures yields higher performance. Yadav et al. used the using VGG16 \& Inception V3 models to investigate enhanced disease detection prediction accuracy of grape leaves\cite{yadav2025enhancing}, comparing deep learning architectures for classification tasks. Khursheed and Kaur presented improved grape leaf disease detection with GAN-augmented ResNet and EfficientNet~\cite{khursheed2025improved}, utilizing generative adversarial networks for data augmentation to address dataset imbalance.


%recent ------
The recent advancement have been focused on transformer-based architectures and hybrid ones. Zhang et al. developed a grape leaf disease recognition using the transformers \cite{zhang2025lightweight}, shows how capable transformer designs are architectures for disease recognition. Li et al. also came with the grape disease detection system using transformer's vision and environmental sensing\cite{li2025grape}, representing a novel approach that fuses visual and environmental data streams. Karthik et al. suggested the idea of GrapeLeafNet which is a dual-track feature fusion network using inception-ResNet and shuffle-transformer for accurate grape leaf disease identification\cite{karthik2024grapeleafnet}, achieving high accuracy scores because of its dual-track feature combination. Another further contribution done by Zhang et al. the cross-resolution transformer for grape leaf diseases segmentaion using context mining\cite{zhang2023crformer}, demonstrating that cross-resolution transformer architecture design enables the precise disease segmentation.


There are some special architectures made for specific detection challenges. Talaat et al. Introduced a DeepLeaf, an optimized deep learning approach for automated recognition of grapevine leaf diseases\cite{talaat2025deepleaf}, which optimize neural architectures for multi-class disease classification. Combining deep learning with manually created features, Atesoglu and Bingol suggested the detection and classification of grape leaf diseases with enhanced hybrid model based on feature engineering\cite{atesoglu2025detection}.Combining deep feature extraction with traditional classifiers in hybrid techniques can produce competitive performance, as demonestrated by Ishengoma and Lyimo's ensemble model for grape leaf disease detection using CNN feature extractors and random forest classifier\cite{ishengoma2024ensemble}. Using grape leaf disease identification with sparse data via GAN and CNN, Chen and Wu addressed data scarcity by means of GAN-based data augmentation to achieve the precision agriculture metrics\cite{chen2023grape}.

The integrating object detection frameworks have made it possible to simultaneously localize and classify plant diseases in complex field environments. The YOLOv7 was used by Yang et al. to study the detection of small lesions on grape leaves \cite{yang2024detection}, and he addressed the difficulty spotting the early-stage disease symptoms. Through architectural modifications, Chen et al. developed an enhanced lightweight grape leaf disease detection method \cite{chen2024yolov8}, therefore improving it performance. Ji et al. carried out a research on grape leaf disease detection method based on NMA-YOLOv8n by introducing a novel attention mechanisms to YOLO architectures\cite{ji2025research}. Particularly in relation to boundary detection issues in the situations of dense foliage, Zhou and Yu suggested a lightweight grape leaf disease identification for complex field scenes–YOLO-Grape model and its boundary-lesion coalesce optimization policies\cite{zhou2025lightweight}.



The next focus has been given to domain-specific challenges including multi-disease detection and disease severity evaluation. Mehta et al.  investigated the grape leaf disease severity analysis using federated learning with CNN techniques\cite{mehta2023grape},  hence bringing privacy-preserving distributed learning for disease assessment.Combining spatial and temporal data for dynamic disease monitoring, Gaur et al. worked on fighting grape black rot with deep learning using CNN-LSTM hybrid model for disease severity classification\cite{gaur2023fighting}. In a review of advancements in deep learning for accurate classification of grape leaves and diagnosis of grape diseases\cite{kunduracioglu2024advancements},Kunduracioglu and Pacal chronicled the change from binary classification to multi-class, multi-disease detection systems. Many other contributions include work by Naik et al. on disease detection in grape leaves\cite{naik2025disease}, Shetty and Shetty on grape leaf disease detection using deep learning approach"\cite{shetty2025grape}, and Liu and Li on grape leaf disease classification based on convolutional neural networks"\cite{liu2025grape}. These all contributions represent the ongoing evolution of deep learning techniques for grape leaf disease detection, with a clear trend towards more sophisticated architectures, multi-disease detection capabilities and consideration of real-world complexities.

\section{Lightweight and Edge-Based Grape Leaf Disease Detection Models}
\label{sec:lightweight_edge_grape_leaf_disease}

% Paragraph 1: Foundation & Introduction
The practical deployment of deep learning models for detecting grape leaf diseases in vineyard fields requires considering the constraints of available computational resources. Even though they achieved high performance in detecting grape leaf diseases, the deployment of these models on resource-constrained edge devices remains challenging. Hence, some researchers focused on developing lightweight neural network architectures specifically designed for edge deployment.

The deployment of deep learning models on the resource-constrained edge devices, at sensor level, is crucial step towards practical, real-time agricultural monitoring systems. Lin et al. started this research direction by developing a model named GrapeNet, a lightweight convolutional neural network model for identification of grape leaf diseases~\cite{lin2022grapenet}. Their work demonstrated that optimized design of lightweight architectures while maintaining acceptable accuracy and significantly reducing computational requirements. This study established a foundation for "the lightweight convolutional neural networks" as a key design principle for edge deployment in resources-constrained applications. 


% Paragraph 2: Mobile-Optimized Architectures
Subsequently, research started to focus neural networks architectures designed and optimized for edge computing. Particularly MobileNet family, due to their depthwise separable convolutions and efficiently designed architectures to reduce computational complexity and make them suitable for resource-constrained devices. Sahid and Cahyadi developed image classification using MobileNet architecture for grape leaf disease detection\cite{sahid2025image}, in a reduced parameter count and computational complexity. Meanwhile, Mathew et al. explored a real-time detection of grape leaf diseases with MobileNetV2 deep learning network\cite{mathew2025real}, achieving real-time inference capabilities on edge hardware. Further improvements introduced attention mechanisms to MobileNetV2 architecture, while maintaining its lightweight characteristics, as shown by Dharrao et al.\cite{dharrao2025efficient}. Hybrid approach, combining MobileNetV2 with transformer architectures have also been explored to increase  performance while keeping the model lightweight\cite{tanvir2025pcnn}.


% Para3: Specialized Lightweight Architectures

Specialized lightweight architectures have been explicitly designed for resource-constrained edge device deployment. This research direction is initiated by Deepa et al. who introduced L-Net, lightweight CNN framework for sustainable multicrop leaf disease detection and classification on edge devices\cite{deepa2026net}. Their work highlighted importance lightweight architectural designs beyond MobileNet. Parallelly, Zhang et al. explored a lightweight grape leaf disease recognition method based on transformer frameworks\cite{zhang2025lightweight}, demonstrating that even transformer architectures can also effectively compress for edge deployment without significant reduction in accuracy.

%  Para4: Practical Edge Deployment Platforms (Raspberry Pi, Jetson, Smartphones)
Since these works showed the effectiveness of lightweight designs, the integration of edge computing with practical deployment platforms has gained significant momentum. Tao et al. developed iCrop+, an edge-boosted crop disease detection system via TinyML and LoRa communication\cite{tao2025icrop+}, demonstrating the integration of TinyML-based image segmentation on Raspberry Pi with LoRa communication for crop disease monitoring. Their system employs TinyML for edge-based image segmentation, followed by CNN inference on the segmented images, with results transmitted over LoRa communication, as shown in figure \ref{fig:icrop}. This work represents a significant step towards practical disease monitoring by integrating edge inference with low-power wireless communication, though deployment remains on single-board computers rather than microcontroller-class devices. Karim et al. also presented a landmark study on enhancing agriculture through real-time grape leaf disease classification via an edge device with a lightweight CNN architecture and Grad-CAM\cite{karim2024enhancing}, demonstrating that lightweight CNN architectures can deploy and perform inference on resource-constrained edge devices. proceeding this work, Lazcano-García et al. developed a deep learning-based system for early symptoms recognition of grapevine red blotch and leafroll diseases and its implementation on edge computing devices\cite{lazcano2025deep}, specifically addressing early disease detection on embedded platforms.

% Paragraph 5: Microcontroller-Class Deployment C
More recently, some researchers start investigating microcontroller-level deployment, in the most resource-constrained scenario. Grujev et al.\cite{grujev2025efficient} demonstrated real-time apple disease classification approach using lightweight neural network on an Arduino edge device. Similarly, Amirkhanova et al.\cite{amirkhanova2026lightweight} developed a lightweight, end-to-end encrypted data pipeline for IoT. Here they explored ESP32 for secure communication in IoT, but they did not use it for visual based inference. 

% Para 6: Model Optimization Techniques
Another, model compression techniques have become crucial enablers for edge deployment. Quantization and pruning are common techniques to compress models. Joshi examined edge-AI for agriculture using lightweight vision models for disease detection in resource-limited settings\cite{joshi2024edge}, emphasizing that Edge AI systems requires careful balance between model accuracy and computational efficiency.

\section{Research Gaps on the Existing LoRaWAN-based Vineyard Monitoring System}
\label{sec:precision_ag_wsn}
%Para 1: IoT-LoRaWAN Gap (Environmental vs. Visual)

Despite the significant advancement progresses in both WSNs for vineyard monitoring and deep learning for grapevine disease detection, a crucial gap, combining them for practical, real-time disease detection still exists. Existing LoRaWAN-based vineyard monitoring systems, also demonstrated by Hnatiuc et al.\cite{hnatiuc2023intelligent}, Gawande et al.\cite{gawande2024early}, and Almufareh et al.~\cite{almufareh2024intelligent}, primarily focuses on environmental sensing. While these systems successfully transmit temperature, humidity, and atmospheric data over LoRaWAN networks, and they can provide indirect indicators of disease risk rather than direct visual based disease diagnosis. 


%Para 2: Deep Learning Gap (Computational Constraints)
Conversely, the substantial body of work on deep learning for grape leaf disease detection\cite{karthik2024grapeleafnet, zhang2023crformer, talaat2025deepleaf}, they achieved high accuracy under unconstrained computational environments. However, these models demands large computational resources, making them unsuitable for microcontroller level deployment.


%Para 3: Lightweight Edge Limitations (Platform)
Although lightweight and edge-based disease detection~\cite{karim2024enhancing, lazcano2025deep, deepa2026net}, have moved towards the embedded level deployment, like in Raspberry Pi, NVIDIA Jetson, and smartphones. However, these devices still consumes high power, so they cannot fit into the LoRaWAN-based vineyard monitoring system.



%Para 4: General End-to-End Integration Gap
Moreover, the integration of complete inference pipelines, including image capturing, object detection, disease detection, and result aggregation on battery powered microcontroller within LoRaWAN networks remains unexplored. The existing efforts are either perform classification on pre-cropped images~\cite{kirubakaran2024grape}, rely on Raspberry Pi-level hardware~\cite{tao2025icrop+}, or assume smartphone-class resources~\cite{mamun2025grape}.

%Para 6: Detection-Classification Integration
Beyond the broader integration challenges, the technical deployment of both leaf localization and  disease detection models into a single microcontroller is not investigated yet. Existing YOLO-based approaches for grape leaf detection\cite{yang2024detection, chen2024yolov8, ji2025research}, have focused on detection detecting the disease directly. They did not explore the integration of a two-stage pipeline where a lightweight model performs leaf localization followed by a separate classifier for disease classification. Similarly, Zhou and Yu\cite{zhou2025lightweight} addressed leaf boundary detection in complex scenes but did not extend their work to additional model integration or wireless communication.



In summary, the core research gap addressed by this thesis is the lack of integrated, edge AI computing systems with capability of performing visual based grape leaf disease detection integrated into the existing LoRaWAN-based vineyard monitoring systems. Thus, this thesis work contributed in addressing this problem by developing Edge AI inference pipeline using ESP32-S3 microcontroller, camera, lightweight YOLO leaf detection model, MobileNetV2 multicalass disease classifier, under LoRaWAN communication constraints. Also duty-cycled operation with deep sleep power management is implemented to ensure long-term deployment feasibility.
